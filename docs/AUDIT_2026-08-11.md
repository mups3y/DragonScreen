## A. Flight-stopping

### A1 · The launch-window hold was pasted into `Engage`, not `Tick`
`AutoPilot.cs:128`. The block sits inside `Engage()`, so it runs **once** and `return`s out of
engagement. Three consequences, all confirmed by absence in the log:

* the hold never happens — the ascent lit while the window said 115 s
* **`BoosterRecovery.PrepareForSeparation(v)` is never called** — 0 occurrences of "physics range
  raised" in the log, against 1 on the previous flight
* `liftoffUt`/`liftoffLonDeg` are never set, so the ascent self-calibration measures from zero, and
  "autopilot ENGAGED" is never logged (0 occurrences)

### A2 · The booster was lost because of A1
*"no booster to recover … the physics range must be raised BEFORE separation"* at MECO, then
*"booster lost after 217 s"*. The recovery code is fine — the flip finished **0.0° off flat
retrograde**, which is the fix from this morning working. It simply drove out of physics range.
**A1 is the whole cause; there is no separate booster bug in this flight.**

### A3 · `OrbitMatch` measures one error and corrects a different one — it cannot converge
`pure/Phasing.cs:196`. `Needed()` tests `|ourSma − stationSma|` against 500 m. The correction,
`CirculariseAtApoapsisDv`, drives our SMA to **our own apoapsis radius**. Those are different
targets, so once circular the test stays true forever.

Its own comment states the assumption: *"the ascent already put our apoapsis within a few hundred
metres of the station's radius"*. This flight inserted at 86.0 km against a station at ~86.75 km —
**750 m, already outside the tolerance on the very first attempt.**

Worse than not converging, it **diverges**: each pass leaves a 0.04–0.05 m/s residual and three
burns logged *"burned past the node"*, so apoapsis creeps up every lap.

> 1643 → 970 → 721 → 593 → *(phasing)* → 3114 → 5369 → 5983 → 6318 → 6472 → 6774 → 7092 → 7460 →
> 7892 → 8197 → 8519 → 8923 → 9217 → 9532 → 9937 → 10235 → 10562 → 11000 → 11312 → 11630 → 12052 →
> 12469 → 12733 → 12986 m

**There is no attempt counter, no convergence test and no give-up path anywhere in the ladder.**

### A4 · The direct approach was locked out at exactly the range it exists for
`StationApproach.cs:176` reads `Leg != ApproachLeg.MatchOrbit`. At 4.4 km with an SMA mismatch the
leg **is** `MatchOrbit`, so the branch never ran. The comment I wrote three lines above it says F9I
gates this *"at stDirectMax and nowhere else"* — and then the code adds two more conditions. Had it
run, the flight would have covered 4.4 km in about three minutes.

### A5 · `NodeExecutor` is a global with no owner, and a second consumer adopts the first's burn
The crew's escape attempt logged
`PHASE-DOWN - LOWERING PERIAPSIS - Holding orbit match`.
`PhaseDownOps` was reporting **StationApproach's** burn as its own: it checks `NodeExecutor.Active`,
finds true, and waits — for a burn it never planned, forever. Five subsystems share one static
executor and none of them declares ownership.

### A6 · Nothing arbitrates between subsystems
`StationApproach` and `DeorbitOps` were both engaged, both driving `AttitudeController.Ascent` and
both wanting `NodeExecutor`. `FlightDriver` ticks all of them unconditionally; there is not one
`Engaged` cross-check in it.

### A7 · No way out
`DeorbitOps.Engage` refuses when already engaged — the idempotency guard I added this morning. It
has no cancel, and `FlightCommands` maps the button to `Engage()`, not `Toggle()`. **The one control
the crew reached for to stop a runaway did nothing.** That guard fixed a real double-press bug and
created a worse one.

### A8 · Warp is unbounded
11.7 hours requested in a 20-minute session, in ~25-minute blocks, at up to 100×. `NodeExecutor`
warps to any ignition however far away, and `WarpWorthwhileS` only sets a floor. A burn an orbit away
is a signal the *plan* is wrong, not an instruction to skip half an hour.

## B. Wrong but survivable

### B1 · Two of three mission buttons are unreachable
Arithmetic, not the photograph: `ChromeBar.Height` is 64, so the tab bar starts at y=639 on a
703-high screen. The buttons land at 581–615 (fine), **623–657** and **665–699** — the last two sit
under the tab bar. My own test asserted `y + rh <= h` (703) instead of `<= h − ChromeBar.Height`,
and `StepRect`'s comment three functions away records the same mistake being caught before.

### B2 · "upper stage has gone on rails" fires on the launch pad
`v.packed` is true for a frame at scene load. Harmless, but it is a warning that means something
specific and alarming, printed at a moment when it is false.

### B3 · The ascent log prints a full state line every 2 s
~150 lines per flight before anything interesting happens. The recorder already has all of it at
5 Hz in columns.

## The plan, in order

Each step ends with something provable. **No new capability until step 4 passes in flight** — the
pattern this session has established is that I add reach faster than I add proof.

### 1 · Un-break what this morning broke  *(A1, A7, B1)*
Small, certain, and it restores a booster recovery that is otherwise finished.

1. Move the pad hold out of `Engage` into `Tick`, after the packed check. Engage must fall through
   to `PrepareForSeparation` unconditionally.
2. `DeorbitNow` → `DeorbitOps.Toggle()`. Second press **cancels**, and says so.
3. `MissionRect` measured up from `ChromeBar.TopY`, not down from `AutoRect`; fix the test to assert
   against the tab bar, not the page.
4. Regression test: engaging the autopilot always calls `PrepareForSeparation`, hold or no hold.

### 2 · Make the rendezvous terminate  *(A3, A4, A8)*
The flight died here and it is one law plus one gate.

1. `OrbitMatch` must correct the error it measures: target the **station's SMA**, not our apoapsis
   radius — a two-burn Hohmann when they differ, and `Needed` false when a single burn cannot close.
2. Give the ladder a **budget**: attempts and total Δv. Exceeded → stop, log, hold, tell the crew.
   *No mission phase may loop without a bound.*
3. Delete the `MatchOrbit` and `NodeExecutor.Active` conditions from the direct-approach gate —
   distance and nothing else, as F9I says twice.
4. Cap a single warp at one orbital period, and refuse to warp at all inside the direct gate.
5. Headless: drive the ladder from the geometry of **this flight** (4.4 km, 750 m SMA error) and
   assert it reaches the goal in a bounded number of burns. That test fails today.

### 3 · One owner per actuator  *(A5, A6)*
The structural fix, and the one that stops this class of failure recurring.

1. `NodeExecutor.Begin` takes an **owner token**; `Active`, `Phase` and `Note` are only readable by
   the owner. A second caller gets a refusal, not somebody else's burn.
2. A single `MissionPhase` arbiter: ascent, rendezvous, docking, undock and return are **mutually
   exclusive**. Engaging one refuses or cleanly stops the other, and `FlightDriver` ticks the one
   that holds the token.
3. Headless: engaging two phases at once must refuse, and a phase must never observe a burn it did
   not plan.

### 4 · Fly it, and only then continue
A launch to insertion plus a booster landing. Read `b_phase`, `b_predMissKm`, `m_rndz`. §4 is fully
ported and audited and **has still never landed once** — that is the outstanding proof, not more
code.

### 5 · Housekeeping  *(B2, B3)*
Suppress the packed warning below the atmosphere; drop the 2 s ascent line to phase transitions only.

## What this session should have done differently

Three of the eight flight-stopping faults — A1, A4, A7 — were **introduced today**, each by a change
that was correct in isolation and untested against the sequence it sat in. The launch-window hold was
pasted into the wrong method; the direct-approach gate contradicted the comment directly above it;
the double-press guard removed the only abort. All three would have been caught by one launch-to-
insertion run before moving on.

`docs/PORT_PLAN.md` rule 0 says a phase with no caller is not ported. It needs a companion:

> **A phase that has not been flown is not finished, and a fix that has not been flown is not a fix.**
