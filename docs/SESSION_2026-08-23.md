# Session 2026-08-23 — ROOT CAUSE of "the two orbits make an X"

## The complaint
User quit a test: "obviously not launching to the correct orbit", screenshot showing our orbit and
the ISS orbit crossing in a big X (KSP AN/DN readout ~86°). Prior sessions had been tuning
`AscentInclinationBiasDeg` — a **red herring** for this: that bias corrects a fraction of a degree of
inclination; the X was the whole **plane (RAAN) ~90° off**, intermittently.

## The bug (PROVEN, not guessed)
`LaunchWindowOps.SecondsToPlaneCrossing` chose the launch instant as `min(tN, tS)` — the **sooner** of
the two plane crossings — with a comment claiming "the node choice no longer matters for the plane
match." That comment is **wrong**.

The ascent **always** flies the ascending (northeast, ~42°) azimuth (`LaunchAzimuth.InertialHeadingDeg`
returns the ascending node solution only). A NE launch is coplanar with the station at **only one** of
the two crossings. At the other, the RAAN comes out ~180° off and the two orbits cross at ~89°.
`min(tN,tS)` grabbed the wrong crossing whenever it fell first → the intermittent X.

**Proof:** `scratchpad/plane_node.py` builds the actual `r × v` orbit of a NE launch at each crossing
and measures relative inclination to the station, swept over all RAAN and pad longitudes:
- coplanar (rel-inc <5°) crossing is **always** the falling-dot one (`PlaneWindow` `north=false`)
- the other crossing gives ~89° — matches the screenshot
- **0 failures** over the whole sweep.

## The fix
`SecondsToPlaneCrossing` now **measures** the NE-launch relative inclination at each crossing
(`NeLaunchRelIncDeg`, new helper) and picks the coplanar one — self-correcting regardless of
frame/spin-sign/naming, and logs both rel-incs + a warning if the chosen one isn't ~0. So a bad pick
is visible on the ground instead of flown as an X. DLL `733e5c7a`, all suites pass, installed.

## What to verify next flight
KSP.log line `plane window: node-N … (NE rel-inc X) / node-S … (NE rel-inc Y) -> hold … Chosen NE
rel-inc Z deg`. **Z must be ~0.** Then the orbits should be coplanar (no X) and the named-burn
rendezvous can actually close the phase. Only after that is the inclination bias worth re-checking —
it was never the X.

## Still open / unproven (do NOT claim fixed)
- Booster landing-burn spool fix (lead 6.5 s + full-throttle-spool) — sim says stops ~440 m up, unflown.
- Named-burn rendezvous → L-approach → docking — built, headless-tested, never flown coplanar.
