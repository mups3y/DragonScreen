## 2. FIXES SHIPPED

### Controller contention + collision safety (#2, #3) — the core of the flight failure
- **`StationApproach.Disengage()` now releases `DirectApproachOps`** — no more zombie after a crew
  cancel. (`StationApproach.cs`)
- **`DeorbitOps.Engage()` force-disengages every other controller first** (ascent, station approach,
  direct approach, docking, undock) — a de-orbit is the point of no return and must own the vehicle
  alone. Belt-and-suspenders that guarantees no contention however the crew got there. (`DeorbitOps.cs`)
- **Keep-out guard in `DirectApproachOps.Tick()`** (`DirectApproach.cs` constants): inside
  `KeepOutFloorM` = 60 m the approach may only brake; inside `HardAbortM` = 25 m it releases the
  vehicle to the crew. The rendezvous can no longer drive into the station.
- **Verify:** on the next rendezvous the approach either matches and hands to docking, or aborts near
  25 m with `KEEP-OUT ABORT` in the log — never a collision. On a de-orbit, `x_owner` should read
  `deorbit` (or the benign `node`), never `CONTENDED:deorbit`; `assess_flight.py` section 6 confirms.

### Camera slide/flicker on load (#1)
- Root cause: the camera was positioned from the **draw path** (`Aim` via `Texture`), which runs
  *after* the depth −102 camera already rendered that frame → one frame stale → slides while the
  vessel is still moving in world space (physics ease-in, before Krakensbane pins it), then "locks in".
- **Fix:** a `DockingCamAimer` MonoBehaviour re-aims the camera in **`OnPreCull`**, immediately before
  it renders. Deliberately **not** parented to the part transform (that would kill the camera at
  staging / dock-merge). (`DockingCamRenderer.cs`)
- **Verify:** cameras should be pinned to the correct spot from the first frame after load — no slide,
  no flicker.

### CANCEL cancels any running sequence (#8)
- `FlightCommands.CancelAllSequences()` stops ascent / rendezvous / direct-approach / docking / undock
  / de-orbit and the interim burn. Wired into the CANCEL button (`PanelButtons.cs`) so it fires whether
  or not a command was armed — but still never punishes a careful press (nothing armed + nothing
  running = stays dark). **Entry is deliberately NOT cancelled** (a committed descent; ChuteGuard still
  deploys chutes independently).

### Landing / entry redesign (#4–#7, #9)

### #12 Booster coast roll — FIXED (`BoosterRecovery.cs`, `AttitudeController.cs`)
- **Your read was right:** it is the control law fighting a **roll reference**. `Aim()` was handing the
  coast an in-plane roll reference (`flipAxis × dir`) on the theory it "induces no roll" — but
  flight_0821_060847 shows **211° of roll on the coast** (2.1× F9I), the weak roll authority chasing that
  reference through the whole ~180° nose-up (the "elephant walk").
- **On the RCS point:** the booster's RCS *is* commanded on for the whole coast (`Landing.cs:830`); the
  `x_rcsOn` recorder column tracks the *capsule*, not the booster, so what you saw "off" on screen was
  the Dragon's indicator. RCS wasn't the lever — the roll *reference* was.
- **Fix:** the coast now drops the reference and sets `LockRoll` — the controller's own **pure rate
  damper** (null the roll rate, chase nothing). This controller's note records it *flown at ~42°* on the
  coast. Gated to the coast only; the descent keeps its reference (needs it once the fins bite), and roll
  doesn't affect the landing point — so the 0.0 km / 1 m/s landing cannot regress. This is an existing,
  flight-tested capability, **not** the tumble-risk `DriveInner` slew.
- **Verify:** coast roll should drop from ~211° toward ~40°; `assess_flight.py` section 4.

## 6. FULLY-PROPULSIVE LANDING · TOURIST MISSION · PRE RANGE (DLL `488a58bb`, UNFLOWN)

### Research: how SpaceX planned propulsive landing
Dragon 2 was originally designed to land *fully propulsively* on 8 SuperDracos (decelerate to zero
velocity, extend legs through the heat shield, touch down), with parachutes as backup; SpaceX switched
to parachute splashdown for crew safety, and in 2024 restored a **drogues-then-SuperDracos** version as
an emergency backup for chute failure. Sources: NASASpaceflight 2024-10, Gizmodo 2024-10.

### "not a kOS port" — confirmed, and rebuilt
The existing propulsive path (`Terminal.cs` from F9I `DgTerminalPropulsive`) is a kOS port that **never
flew in DragonScreen (0/162 flights)** and defaulted off in F9I too (`falcon9.ks:110`). So it was
untested ported code. The crew chose **fully propulsive (no chutes)** — the original SpaceX plan — so I
rebuilt the terminal descent for it rather than copying the drogue-then-burn:
- `EntryOps.AwaitDrogue`: on a propulsive landing, **no drogues** — go straight to lighting the
  SuperDracos (the 5 km propulsive floor leaves time to light, prove, and commit the ~1.6 km hoverslam).
- The hoverslam itself is the **proven booster law** (`Terminal.LandingThrottle` = `Deorbit.LandingThrottle`,
  the same one that lands the booster 0.0 km / 1 m/s) — kept because it *is* flight-proven.
- **Legs out DURING the hoverslam** (`Terminal.GearDeployAltM` = 40 m), through the heat shield, because
  the capsule lands ON them. Guarded so the post-touchdown deploy doesn't double-fire.
- **Engine-failure abort**: if the SuperDracos won't light at the commit point, deploy the (stowed)
  chutes and fall back to a parachute descent — the one place chutes appear on a "no chutes" landing.
- **Mono gate raised 40 → 120 units** (`Terminal.MonoGateUnits`): a no-chute hoverslam kills the whole
  ~120-150 m/s terminal velocity (~200 m/s of dv), not the ~60 m/s a drogue-then-burn did. Below the
  gate it drops back to parachutes. ⚠ ESTIMATE — refine from the recorded landing-burn dv.
- Feasibility: SuperDracos ~228 kN, ~400 m/s of mono in the tank (the mod already circularises on them),
  TWR ~2.6 → a hoverslam is well within reach IF the capsule is refuelled before the return.
- **Verify:** BACKUP entry + STRING 1C → watch for `engines proven lit … committing the hoverslam`,
  `landing gear out at 40 m`, `LANDED — N m from target` on LZ-1, and no chute deploy.
