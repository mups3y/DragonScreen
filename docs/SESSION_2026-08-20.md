# SESSION 2026-08-20g — THE PREDICTOR WAS WRONG: ported Trajectories' stock drag

DLL `cb6459033c8e3aab15ab6c171639511f`, installed, **UNFLOWN**. This is the keystone: the burn, trim
and entry all steer on our impact predictor, and flight_0820_092128 proved it disagrees with reality -
ours said **7.5 km** at burnout, the capsule (flying ballistic) came down **29 km** away, exactly where
Trajectories predicted. Our predictor only converged near the ground.

## Root cause: a single ballistic coefficient can't model an entry
Ours integrated drag as `0.5·rho·v²/bc` with a FIXED bc (440). KSP drag is per-part DRAG CUBES whose
area varies with Mach; a fixed bc is that area frozen at one Mach and massively over-drags at the high
Mach of entry, so the predicted impact lands tens of km short. Trajectories sums each part's
`DragCubes.AddSurfaceDragDirection(..).areaDrag` at the live Mach - that's the whole difference.

## Fix: ported StockAeroUtil.SimAeroForce (fetched the mod's actual source)
- `ImpactPredictor.AeroTable` / `BuildAeroTable`: sum each part's drag-cube areaDrag over a 0..25 Mach
  grid, ONCE, for the SHIELD-FORWARD attitude (airflow = −ReferenceTransform.up in each part's local
  frame - invariant to how the vessel points now, so it's right even while the burn is vectored).
  Trunk/second-stage excluded (jettisoned before entry). Mass in TONNES (KSP force is kN → kN/tonne = m/s²).
- `pure/Trajectory.cs`: the RK4 integrator now takes a `DragFactorAt(mach, rhoV)` + `SoundSpeed`
  delegate. Drag accel = `0.5·rho·v²·factor`, factor = `0.001·DragMult·DragCubeMult·pseudoReynolds·
  ΣareaDrag(mach)/mass` - SimAeroForce exactly. Scalar bc kept as the fallback.
- `StockDensity`/`StockSoundSpeed`: ported from the mod (day/night equatorial temp) so Mach matches.
- Applied to the CAPSULE only (callers passing a known bc>0). The BOOSTER's `Predict(v)` is untouched -
  it's happy and its shield-forward geometry differs.

Why it matters: with an accurate predictor, the vectored burn aims the REAL impact at LZ-1 and the
ballistic-hold entry lands where predicted. The wrong predictor is why every recent flight missed
despite the guidance "working". Tests green, WRONG 0, md5 ok. Next flight: our logged known-bc landing
prediction should match the Trajectories red-X and the actual splashdown.

`flipAxis × dir`: ⊥ flipAxis (so it lies in the plane of flight — the launch roll, starboard on flipAxis)
and ⊥ dir (a valid top), and being a cross product it is NEVER parallel to dir → never degenerate.
Holding it pins starboard to flipAxis → every nose move is pitch, never yaws out of plane, never rolls.

**Why the earlier tries failed, and why this doesn't** (all flown, flight_0820_*):
- `upHint = zero` → no reference; SteerTo builds targetTop off the NEW aim while DriveInner scores roll
  vs the OLD forward, so each phase boundary injects a PHANTOM roll error (coast-entry −89.7°) → commanded
  roll. And with no reference, induced roll drifts.
- `-flipAxis` → the plane NORMAL (cross-track). Killed the phantom but rolled the FLIP 64–90° (railed at
  15°/s) to drive the top OUT of plane: the launch roll sits ~90° off the normal. `flipAxis × dir` is
  that normal CROSSED with the aim — IN-plane — exactly the launch roll, so it commands no roll to reach.
- `LockRoll` (pure rate damper) → removed commanded roll but a damper can't remove INDUCED roll: coast
  still 42° (kinematic, from a pitch+yaw reorientation), descent −222° (AERODYNAMIC — a roll burst 24→13
  km, omP/omY≈0, stopping when the fins bite). Both are fixed at the source by pitch-only steering: no
  pitch+yaw reorientation to induce the coast roll, and the roll is actively HELD so the fins fight the
  descent aero the moment they bite (user: fins have authority only in atmosphere).

**Sign:** `flipAxis × dir` and its negative are two in-plane tops 180° apart; `slewSign` captures which
matches the stage's actual top, once, when flipAxis is first known — so establishing the reference
commands no roll. Before flipAxis exists (sep quiet), hold current roll (zero).

Also kept: `SingleAxisSlew` (walk the aim about one axis, rate-limited, nose-catch gated) rate-limits the
reorientations under the pitch-only reference. Ascent already holds roll to 0.1–0.6°, so "from launch
pad" needs nothing there. The `LockRoll` field remains in `AttitudeController` but the booster no longer
sets it (held-reference supersedes it).

## m. Three-flight determinism experiment (baselines archived) — DLL c16db9ca

User flew THREE landings from ONE save "to reduce variables and find all bugs". Archived as
flight_0820_175458 / _181043 / _182849. All three start bit-identical (first periapsis drop at
met 336, lon 92.05°, to 114.9 km). What they revealed:

| | fl1 175458 | fl2 181043 | fl3 182849 |
|---|---|---|---|
| deep-burn commit lon | −155.84° | **+172.03°** | −155.94° |
| de-orbit predicted miss at burnout | 63.8 km | 152.8 km | 61.4 km |
| entry-start cross @85 km | −424 m | **+250 m** | −429 m |
| touchdown miss | 594 m | 479 m | 572 m |
| landed vs LZ | 173 S / 569 E | **393 N / 274 W** | 173 S / 546 E |

fl1 and fl3 are the same flight twice. **fl2 committed the de-orbit a whole lap early** (lon +172 vs
−155.9) and landed on the opposite side. That is the headline bug — identical inputs, different
outputs — and it is exactly what the experiment was designed to catch.

### ROOT CAUSE — degenerate pass selection over an equatorial site
`Overflight.Search` picks the pass with the smallest `TrackMissM` (which is the full great-circle
`GroundRange` to the site, not cross-track) across 5 orbits. Over a near-equatorial site the ground
track crosses the LZ once ~every lap, so there are several all-but-equal minima one lap apart. A few
km of orbit jitter — the open-loop phase-down stops at a different periapsis each load (85.7 / 88.7 /
89.2 km, a 3.5 km spread) — tips which lap is marginally smallest. The author already knew the
cross-track term is "noise" on this orbit (`DeorbitOps.cs:580`); the along-track term (which lap = which
longitude) was never in the objective.

### FIX (applied, tested, installed — DLL c16db9ca, needs full KSP restart)
1. **`Overflight.TieMarginM = 3000 m` + coarse sweep `if (m < best - TieMarginM)`** (`pure/Overflight.cs`).
   A later pass must beat the incumbent by a real margin to steal the choice, so near-equal laps
   resolve to the EARLIEST pass every load — the same pass across loads. An honestly better pass (an
   inclined orbit, crossings km apart) still wins by clearing the margin. Refinement kept strict `<`
   (±12 steps stay inside one crossing). New regression in `ReturnPathTest.cs` (twin-minima a lap
   apart: earlier wins, sub-margin wobble does not flip it, a clear win still flips it); the toy `Vee`
   was given a realistic 2280 m/s slope so the margin case is actually exercised. ALL SUITES PASSED.
2. **`assess_flight.py` gating bug**: the `r_` block was gated on `r_stage`, which is the ENTRY stage
   and reads "-" through the whole de-orbit phase — so de-orbit columns (r_deorbitMissKm, r_deorbitThr,
   r_nodePhase, r_nodeDvLeft) were judged only over entry rows where they are frozen, and reported
   "CONSTANT / ALL ZERO". Now gated on `return_active` (r_stage active OR x_owner in deorbit/entry).
   Flags 40 → 35; the 9 that remain (r_method, r_vertCmd, r_latCmd, r_aoaCmd, r_wantLongKm,
   r_belowProfile, r_worstErrKm, r_burnThr, r_bcBooster) are genuinely dead legacy entry-telemetry
   columns the current EntryOps no longer populates — a recorder-schema leftover, not a flight bug.

### NOT changed, on purpose (needs the now-repeatable flight to calibrate)
Biases (`AlongBiasM` 1700 / `CrossBiasM` 310), the ballistic-hold latch (`EntryHoldM` 10 km), the
fixed lead (`PhaseArcFrac`). Every one of these was tuned against flights whose pass was a coin-flip,
so their values are fit to scatter, not signal. The residual on the deterministic pass — fl1/fl3 land
~570 m LONG (east) + ~170 m south, and the entry trim quits at q=0.30 kPa (~50 km) with ~300 m cross
+ ~2 km along still uncorrected — is now a **repeatable systematic offset** that the NEXT flight can
measure cleanly. Read that flight, set the biases once, then decide whether the trim needs to run
deeper. Do NOT touch the de-orbit search again per the standing revert rule.

## n. The save-load divergence is the PHASE-DOWN burn, not the pass search — DLL 4f46ee4f

The crew flew two more from the same save: one landed on target (564 m), one was "way off, trim
made it worse then overshot, burned almost all the mono, eventually crawled back." Asked whether
loading from a save is the bug. It is - and here is exactly where.

| | 191736 (off) | 193553 (on target) |
|---|---|---|
| plateau periapsis after phase-down | **82.1 km** | **88.7 km** |
| de-orbit commit lon | +171° | −156° |
| de-orbit predicted miss at burnout | **187 km** | 60 km |
| entry-start along miss | **270 km** | 28 km |
| mono used | **171 / 195** | 117 |
| touchdown | 642 m | 564 m |

### It is NOT the warp, and NOT the save corrupting data
Fine trace of `a_ctlThrottle` at the burn tail (fine2.py): the phase-down periapsis-lowering burn
**tapers to zero on its own** and warp only begins AFTER.
- 191736: throttle → 0.00 at met 361.8, **peri 82.18 km**; warp starts met 362.1.
- 193553: throttle → 0.00 at met 357.2, **peri 88.75 km**; warp starts met 357.8.
Same identical tapering burn (both start bit-identical at met 334), but 193553's low-thrust Draco
tail crosses zero ~5 s sooner and stops **6.5 km higher**. Target periapsis is 79.2 km - BOTH
undershoot, by different amounts, and `PhaseDownOps.BurnOne` accepted the first result and moved on.
Loading from a save changes the sub-frame timing that decides where that tail terminates, so the same
save yields a landing orbit whose periapsis wanders ~7 km (82-89 km across five flights).

### Why that 7 km is a landing-scale error
A 7 km periapsis change is a different orbital period, and the (near-degenerate, equatorial) de-orbit
pass search reads it as a different orbit and commits a whole pass away - +171° vs −156°. That is a
different entry geometry: 270 km of along-track for the trim to claw back vs 28 km. The trim was not
broken; it was handed 270 km it had no business seeing, so it ran full-throttle and burned the tank.
The "on target" flight is what the system does when the phase-down happens to land near-target.

### Why the earlier TieMargin fix was necessary but not sufficient
`Overflight.TieMarginM` makes the search deterministic FOR A GIVEN orbit (within-run near-ties). This
is an ACROSS-run divergence: the orbit itself differs between loads, so the search legitimately
returns different global minima. The seed had to be fixed upstream.

### FIX (applied, tested, installed - DLL 4f46ee4f, needs full KSP restart)
`PhaseDownOps.BurnOne` now **tops up**: after the executor finishes, if periapsis is still above
`TargetPeriapsisM + ToleranceM` it clears `burnLaunched` and plants a fresh lowering node next tick,
up to `MaxPeriapsisTopUps = 3`, then proceeds regardless (out-of-propellant still ends). The periapsis
converges to a repeatable 79.2 ± 1.5 km every load, so the de-orbit is handed the same orbit each
time. Burn 2 (apoapsis) was already consistent (95.06 vs 95.09) and is left alone. Tests green.

⚠ **This changes the flown orbit.** Before, the capsule flew 95 × (82-89); now it will fly a
repeatable 95 × ~79 km. The NEXT flight's landing offset is the real, calibratable one - biases must
be re-read from it, not carried over. Do NOT touch burn 2 or the de-orbit search.
