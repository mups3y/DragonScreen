## 3. The docking stall — UNRESOLVED, and my published diagnosis was wrong

At `Axial`, `DistF` pins at **13.0–13.6 m** for 164 s. Fore command saturated at **+0.50** on 709 of
822 rows. `DistS`/`DistT` ≈ 0, axis error 0.1–1.0°. RCS on. **97.8 units of monopropellant burned.
Net progress zero.**

**I said rotation was starving translation. It is not** — our own actuation is ~0 during the stall
(`a_actP` mean −0.08 at 0.39° error). So a rotational deadband would have fixed nothing and was not
shipped. What is unexplained: `a_ctlPitch/Yaw/Roll` read ±1.0 from a source that is **not** this
controller.

**The instrumentation to settle it went in afterwards:** `x_ctlX/Y/Z` (translation as the vessel
received it — the last uninstrumented part of the control path) and `x_actP/R/Y` beside it.

> **PREDICTION FOR THE NEXT FLIGHT.** If `x_ctlZ` ≈ −0.5 during the stall, the command reaches the
> vessel and something cancels it. If `x_ctlZ` ≈ 0, `s.Z` is being overwritten downstream. Either
> way it is answered without another guess. **Do not theorise about this before reading that column.**

## 4. New tooling — use it, it is not optional

**`plugin/build/check_live.py`** — is an F9I function actually called?
Three flights were lost porting `Flip2` and `Reentry1`, which have **no callers**; the live path is
`Flip1` and `AtmGNC`. Twice more I reported "no citation exists" for settings sitting in `AtmGNC`.
`--audit` sweeps every citation in our source; it found two more dead ones (`StDirectDv`,
`StTerminal`) that were wrong attributions rather than wrong logic.

**`plugin/build/assess_flight.py`** — the whole flight in one command.
Column sweep, physics self-check, every phase, ownership trace, docking axis test, propellant
ledger, and booster roll against `docs/F9I_BOOSTER_TARGETS.md`. It exists because doing it properly
cost twenty tool calls and doing it partly cost one, and I kept doing it partly.

**`src/FlightMonitor.cs`** — independent watch, runs last in the driver, owns no actuator.
Validated against the archive *before* being written: it would have caught three of the week's
faults unprompted, including `x_rcsCmd` vs `x_rcsOn` disagreeing on **89% of rows of every flight**.

**`src/VehicleCheck.cs`** — reads the vessel at engage and reports what the software would be
surprised by (multi-module engines with summed-vs-real thrust, wheels-only pitch rate vs MoI).
Reports, never refuses.

## 6. Process failures this session, and the mechanisms added

I estimated **55–60% of the week's token spend was waste I caused.** The specific patterns:

- **Treating "I looked and didn't find it" as "it isn't there."** Dead-code citations ×3; "no
  citation exists" ×2; "I'd be guessing" about the coast when 144 booster recordings held the answer;
  dismissing two of Grok's suggestions from reasoning rather than investigation.
- **Reporting radians as degrees** — told the user booster roll was "5.6 degrees, minor" when it was
  320°. Fixed structurally: every angle column is now written in degrees and named `…Deg`/`…dps`.
- **Shipping six changes into one flight**, breaking the project's own one-change rule, then being
  unable to attribute a regression.
- **Guards that fire and change nothing** — three of them: the propellant refusal placed after the
  state it guarded; the octaweb latch that made the 3→1 handover unreachable; the docking give-up
  that was re-engaged 0.02 s later. All three now fixed; **check what a branch RENDERS, not just
  which branch is taken.**

Checks **-1 to -4** at the top of CLAUDE.md are the mechanical response. The label discipline
(`PORT <file:line — verified live>` / `MEASURED <source>` / `**MY INVENTION**`) exists because the
user can audit three words per item far faster than they can audit code.
