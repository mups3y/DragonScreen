## 3. The docking stall - SOLVED 2026-08-12 (flight 19:29), and it was arithmetic

**The prediction was answered.** `x_ctlZ` = exactly `-fore` on every row, so the command reached the
vessel intact and nothing downstream cancelled it. **Both of my hypotheses were wrong** - it was
never the control path. It was the MEASUREMENT.

`FlyTo` resolves `to = target - ourPos`, and `target` is **the current waypoint, not the port**.
Every stage test is proximity to its own waypoint, so each stage falsifies its own entry condition
by succeeding. `AtStandoff` is "within 12 m of a point 25 m out along the axis", so at 13 m from the
port Axial's condition goes false BECAUSE AXIAL WORKED, the latched corridor branch takes over, and
its target is 25 m back out.

Measured, one tick, met 447.78 -> 447.98:

    stage        Axial   ->  Corridor
    x_dkDistF   +13.85   ->  -11.35      a 25.20 m step  (StandoffM = 25.0)
    x_dkRangeM   14.40   ->   14.22      continuous - the vessel moved 0.18 m
    fore cmd      0.00   ->    -0.50

**The stall range is StandoffM - StandoffToleranceM = 25 - 12 = 13 m**, and both flights pinned at
13.0-13.6 m. Not a coincidence, a constant.

**FIXED: the stage machine is now MONOTONE.** `reached` is a high-water mark; a stage is entered if
its test passes OR the approach has already been there. `inCorridor` was the same bug found once and
latched once - a second latch would have fixed Axial and left `Rounding` to do it a third time.
ToGate and Rounding share a rank on purpose (rounding is a continuous curve, not a milestone).
Going backwards is not a state to recover from; that is the no-progress guard's job.

**ALSO FIXED: the recorded geometry froze during a coast.** `DistF/S/T` and `VelF/S/T` were written
only on the path that also commands thrust, so `c.Coast` left the last commanded tick's numbers
looking current - `x_dkDistF` held 11.64 for 8 s while the true range fell 24.7 -> 17.4 m. They are
a measurement, not a command, and are now taken every tick before any early return.

*Proof for the next flight:* `x_dkDistF` never changes sign within one approach, and
`stages reached` includes `Docked`.

## 3a. The entry emptied the tank - one fix, and two things deliberately NOT changed

57 of 185 mono units between 37 km and 11 km, reaching zero with 234 s still to fly. `ctlYaw` pinned
at -1.00 from met 3901, `a_tgtOmegaYdps` running away to -26.7 deg/s against an actual 0.05, and
`tgtTorqueY` clipped at exactly the available torque.

**The AoA law is a FAITHFUL PORT and is not the bug.** I assumed we had ported Trajectories'
prediction rows as a steering schedule; reading `EntryOps:446-457` shows we do exactly what
`dragon_deorbit.ks:2312-2320` does - lift magnitude scales the AoA, and zero lift collapses to
exactly retrograde. What actually happened: `missM` was 84 km ALONG-track, `vertCmd` stayed 0.00
(the range loop can only shorten, and we were short), `latCmd` railed at 1.00, and the capsule held
a full 15 deg of yaw-plane AoA against the airstream for 350 s.

**FIXED - the landing reserve now outranks the range in the lifting entry too.** `Trim()` already
refuses to spend below `Reserve()` and its own comment calls that "the same test the de-orbit burn
loop uses so the two cannot disagree". The lifting entry was the one caller that did not apply it.
Chute reserve is 12 units.

**NOT CHANGED 1 - `EntryGuidance.YawSign`.** Cross-track grew 2.8 km -> 12.0 km under a railed
lateral command, which is exactly this project's own test for an inverted axis. It is still not
enough. F9I's `dgPitchSign`/`dgYawSign` comments say "entry re-identifies it live" - **that comment
is STALE, the auto-identifier is retired, and the reason is written at `dragon_deorbit.ks:396-401`:
"it misread an uncontrollable error as a wrong sign and flipped twice on 004".** An 84 km
along-track error at L/D 0.27 is uncontrollable, so this flight cannot decide the sign, and flipping
it here would repeat 004 exactly. `dgSignWin / dgSignDead / dgSignMaxFlip` are dead - do not port
them. Decide the sign on a flight whose along-track error is small.

**NOT CHANGED 2 - `Deorbit.AimDracoCrew`.** Still 284 400. The constant's own comment says ONE
FLIGHT, ONE FIT, and this flight cannot re-fit it: no ascent leg, docking failed and changed the
mass and propellant at de-orbit, and the de-orbit ran the node-executor path. Re-fitting here would
be a second single-point fit on worse data.

**NOT A BUG - `r_trimErrM` "frozen".** I reported it constant at -57376.2. It ran under time warp:
8-15 s of MET per recorder row, 4 control ticks in the whole stage, and the value moved -57376.1 ->
-57376.9 -> -57376.2. Three samples of a slowly-varying quantity, not a stale cache.

**FIXED - `r_bcAscent` logged 0.0000 for the entire entry.** It asked `AutoPilot.AscentVessel`,
which is null on any flight that did not launch under the autopilot, while `FlightDriver:73` was
sampling `EntryOps.Vehicle` all the way down. One of "the two columns that judge a return", reading
a vessel that did not exist.

## 4. New tooling — use it, it is not optional

**`plugin/build/check_live.py`** — is an F9I function actually called?
Three flights were lost porting `Flip2` and `Reentry1`, which have **no callers**; the live path is
`Flip1` and `AtmGNC`. Twice more I reported "no citation exists" for settings sitting in `AtmGNC`.
`--audit` sweeps every citation in our source; it found two more dead ones (`StDirectDv`,
`StTerminal`) that were wrong attributions rather than wrong logic.

**`plugin/build/assess_flight.py`** — the whole flight in one command.
Column sweep, physics self-check, every phase, ownership trace, docking axis test, propellant
ledger, and booster roll against `docs/F9I_BOOSTER_TARGETS.md`. It exists because doing it properly
cost twenty tool calls and doing it partly cost one, and I kept doing it partly.

**`src/FlightMonitor.cs`** — independent watch, runs last in the driver, owns no actuator.
Validated against the archive *before* being written: it would have caught three of the week's
faults unprompted, including `x_rcsCmd` vs `x_rcsOn` disagreeing on **89% of rows of every flight**.

**`src/VehicleCheck.cs`** — reads the vessel at engage and reports what the software would be
surprised by (multi-module engines with summed-vs-real thrust, wheels-only pitch rate vs MoI).
Reports, never refuses.

## 6. Process failures this session, and the mechanisms added

I estimated **55–60% of the week's token spend was waste I caused.** The specific patterns:

- **Treating "I looked and didn't find it" as "it isn't there."** Dead-code citations ×3; "no
  citation exists" ×2; "I'd be guessing" about the coast when 144 booster recordings held the answer;
  dismissing two of Grok's suggestions from reasoning rather than investigation.
- **Reporting radians as degrees** — told the user booster roll was "5.6 degrees, minor" when it was
  320°. Fixed structurally: every angle column is now written in degrees and named `…Deg`/`…dps`.
- **Shipping six changes into one flight**, breaking the project's own one-change rule, then being
  unable to attribute a regression.
- **Guards that fire and change nothing** — three of them: the propellant refusal placed after the
  state it guarded; the octaweb latch that made the 3→1 handover unreachable; the docking give-up
  that was re-engaged 0.02 s later. All three now fixed; **check what a branch RENDERS, not just
  which branch is taken.**

Checks **-1 to -4** at the top of CLAUDE.md are the mechanical response. The label discipline
(`PORT <file:line — verified live>` / `MEASURED <source>` / `**MY INVENTION**`) exists because the
user can audit three words per item far faster than they can audit code.
